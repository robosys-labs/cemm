# Predictive nucleus: assessment and integrity audit

**Status: G1 is not admitted.** This audit covers the isolated research stack
through PR #36. Earlier reports remain historical descriptions of the code and
public development cases actually run; they are not independent challenge or
release evidence. Do not infer a general architecture advantage, calibrated
false-alarm rate, or a Wikipedia utility claim from them.

## Findings and corrective scope

1. **Transition distributions and forecast binding.** The previous belief
   filter could accept an invalid unary transition arity, or normalize away
   errors in different local transition rows. A returned forecast could also
   be mutated independently of the monitor. The corrective implementation
   validates each row before mixing, protects committed snapshots, and checks
   that monitor and belief receipts refer to the same joint forecast, action,
   identity order and supplied calibration. Public inspection of the posterior
   and episode calibration cannot accidentally rewrite their stored values.
   These are in-process integrity
   checks; no historical run is known to have supplied the malformed fixture
   kernels or mutated a forecast. Valid-kernel regression replay is required.

2. **Context support versus the predicted input.** V6's original context guard
   used the latest raw sensor readings, which can differ from the belief state
   being forecast. Its reported coverage/error numbers remain measurements of
   that raw-reading heuristic; they do not prove that the forecast's input
   context was observed. A new opt-in diagnostic reports how much current
   posterior mass lies on previously observed ordered contexts, and compares
   observation and committed-action histories. This is not a calibrated
   probability of correctness or an authenticated episode binding. It does not
   replace the legacy policy or establish a new accuracy result.

3. **Flat baseline sensor metadata.** All reference training and calibration
   episodes supply sensor error rate zero. The flat GRU's 21 metadata-column
   weights therefore receive zero data gradient; nonzero rates at evaluation
   affect the old baseline through untrained initialized weights. The clean,
   bilateral, conditional and switch scenarios use zero metadata and are not
   affected by this specific issue; noisy and miscalibrated baseline comparisons
   must not be interpreted as learned use of sensor reliability. An opt-in
   neutral mode ignores this feature, but is noise-unaware and is not a sensor
   correction. The historical default is retained solely for exact replay.

4. **Log-loss and calibration labels.** Historical `joint_nll` fields apply
   `max(p, 1e-12)` and are clipped at approximately 27.631 nats. They are
   lower bounds on uncapped loss, not uncapped proper log scores. New stress,
   guard and flat score summaries retain the legacy field and additionally
   report floor counts, represented zero probabilities and an uncapped mean;
   a represented zero produces an explicitly tagged infinite mean rather than
   JSON infinity. Numerical underflow can also create a represented zero. The
   older `probe_trial` and `evaluate` scorers remain legacy: their losses are
   clipped, and the probe trial can compare marginal-rounded predictions with
   joint-MAP confidence when computing ECE. Do not call these calibration gates.

5. **Sensor randomness and physical event identity.** V3–V8 derive sensor RNG
   from a learner-visible entity handle; no nominal anytime false-alarm bound
   is established by those deterministic public runs. An allowlisted source
   string is not authentication: a physical reading restamped with a new
   source/time/ID cannot be identified as a replay by this monitor alone. A
   future experiment needs independently custodied sensor randomness and
   authenticated immutable physical-event provenance. An in-process helper,
   public seed, commitment, or checksum by itself cannot provide that custody.

6. **Pairing and baseline comparability.** Existing world generation includes
   the physics family in its seed digest: comparisons between scenarios do not
   share initial worlds and cannot isolate physics as a paired causal effect.
   Within a scenario the paired policies do share their specified trajectory
   where documented. The flat model's training geometry also differs from
   stress evaluation: 1,742 of 2,378 next-step training sensor inputs in the
   600-episode sample were full-world, whereas stress probe feedback is
   restricted to action participants. The structured model has a supplied
   analytic belief and local-effect bias. These differences limit architectural
   interpretation; equal wall time and equal optimizer steps are not equal
   FLOPs, and the flat model replays the structured model's probe policy.

7. **Cross-run reproducibility.** Fixed-update flat training losses and some
   predictions differed between the archived PR #36 CI job and the later audit
   CI job, although their structured results and trajectory hashes matched.
   Same-job fixed-step replay passed. The earlier artifacts do not identify
   the actual CPU model or hash training tensors and weights, so the cause of
   the cross-job difference has not been established. The audit CI compares
   pre-audit and corrected code on the same host with fixed optimizer schedules,
   hashes sensor-only training/calibration tensors and learned weights, and
   records the CPU model and PyTorch configuration. Same-host equality cannot
   establish bitwise reproducibility across hardware or independent jobs.

The new `paired_world.py` is infrastructure only: it creates common initial
conditions across the public physics families. It does not run a scientific
experiment, provide a sensor stream, fix the old V3–V8 harness, authenticate a
physical event or provide independent custody. Public or enumerable world seeds
can reveal the derived hidden orientation through the public generator; the
content identity itself is also not a hiding commitment for a small state
space. A
commitment would not substitute for independent review of the full protocol,
code, world inventory, sensor configuration and secret handling. A proposed
sensor-stream helper was withheld after review identified unresolved integrity
and custody boundaries; it must be designed and reviewed separately.

## Decision and next gate

**HOLD / REVISE**, with all experimental and production promotion gates closed.
Keep the G0–V8 manifests and archived artifacts intact. Before any new
comparative or false-alarm claims: establish immutable event provenance and
independently held sensor randomness; use physics-independent matched initial
worlds and predeclared sensor geometry; specify and freeze fresh evaluation
splits and calibrated scoring; run appropriate controls and review both
regressions and repairs. A public seed or a rerun of an opened development
family is not an independent blind challenge. No work here authorizes G2–G5,
production integration, external effects or a 3× usefulness claim.
